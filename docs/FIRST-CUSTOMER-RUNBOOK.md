# First Customer Delivery Runbook

## Owner and outcome

The PageLoom owner owns the commercial relationship and every external commitment. The delivery outcome is an approved website, deployed to Firebase Hosting, with an acceptance record, recoverable release, and named support path.

## 1. Before the call

- Confirm the offer, price, included pages, revision limit, target timeline, payment milestones, and exclusions.
- Prepare the customer agreement, privacy notice, accessibility commitment, and data-processing terms for owner/legal approval.
- Confirm the customer has a Google account they control for secure portal access.
- Do not promise a production date until the AI-provider health check and release capacity are green.

## 2. Close the deal

- The owner completes the phone call and records the lead as Closed Won with evidence.
- Record the agreed scope, price, currency, payment status, deadline, decision-maker, and primary contact.
- AI work must not start before Closed Won is recorded.
- Use a manual invoice and payment-confirmation process for customer #1; payment-provider automation is intentionally deferred.

## 3. Onboard

- Create portal access from CRM for the exact verified Google email supplied by the customer.
- Ask the customer to sign in; confirm that only their project, questionnaire, files, comments, and approvals are visible.
- Generate the questionnaire, review required fields, and send the customer the portal URL only after owner approval of that external message.
- Verify uploaded logo, brand assets, photography, legal text, contact details, service details, and ownership/usage rights.

## 4. Produce

- Validate assets before orchestration starts.
- Confirm every stage produces the deliverables listed by the workflow definition.
- Review business research, competitor research, brand strategy, design system, sitemap, UX plan, UI, copy, SEO, source, and build evidence in sequence.
- Record every retry, rollback, rejection, and manual intervention in project history.
- If AI inference is unavailable, pause the workflow; never silently substitute fabricated output.

## 5. Quality gate

- Production build succeeds from a clean install and locked dependencies.
- No broken internal links, missing images, dead forms, console errors, or accidental secrets.
- Keyboard navigation, visible focus, landmarks, labels, contrast, and reduced-motion behavior pass review.
- Mobile, tablet, desktop, Hebrew RTL, and English layouts pass visual review for the agreed scope.
- Titles, descriptions, canonical URLs, robots policy, sitemap, structured data, headings, and social metadata are correct.
- Security headers, tenant boundaries, upload restrictions, and owner-only deployment approval pass verification.
- Backup and rollback evidence references the release candidate.

## 6. Approval and deployment

- Capture CEO approval of the exact release candidate.
- Capture customer approval or documented requested revisions.
- Re-run the full quality gate after revisions.
- Request explicit owner approval immediately before production deployment.
- Deploy the approved immutable release, run health checks, and record URL, release ID, timestamp, approver, and verification evidence.

## 7. Handoff

- Give the customer the production URL, approved scope summary, known limitations, content-update process, support channel, and response target.
- Record domain/DNS ownership, Firebase project ownership, analytics ownership, and any third-party account responsibilities.
- Obtain final acceptance and mark the project complete only after all deliverables are accessible.
- Schedule a 48-hour check and a 14-day follow-up. Request a testimonial only with owner-approved external communication.

## Stop conditions

Stop and request owner action for billing changes, Google authorization, production deployment, legal acceptance, payments, domains, destructive operations, email, or WhatsApp sends.
