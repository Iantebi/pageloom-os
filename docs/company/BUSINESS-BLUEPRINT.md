# PageLoom business blueprint

Owner: CEO · Review: quarterly · Effective: draft pending owner adoption

## Identity

**Vision:** Every small business can own a fast, trustworthy, accessible digital presence without managing a fragmented agency process.

**Mission:** PageLoom converts owner-approved customer knowledge into measurable website deliverables through a Hebrew-first, traceable production system.

**Core values:** customer truth over persuasion; owner accountability; quality before volume; secure isolation; accessible design; evidence-based decisions; reversible operations; transparent costs; automation with human control.

## Market and value

Primary customers are Israeli small and medium service businesses that need a brochure or lead-generation website and value a guided Hebrew service. The buying user is normally the business owner. PageLoom is not positioned for regulated enterprise workloads or complex commerce until the corresponding compliance and operations are proven.

**Unique value proposition:** one accountable process takes a customer from structured discovery to a tested, deployed, maintainable website, with every deliverable, approval, revision, and cost traceable.

Competitive advantages are Hebrew-first delivery, a repeatable website factory, manual owner control at critical gates, isolated customer data, reusable design and delivery standards, and provider-independent AI task preparation. AI never closes a sale or performs a protected external action.

## Business model

Revenue comes from fixed-scope website setup, recurring hosting and maintenance, approved add-ons, and separately scoped changes. Revenue is recognized from actual ledger entries—not pipeline. The company favors sustainable gross margin and predictable recurring service over discount-led volume.

Growth proceeds in gates: founder-led first customers; repeatable delivery and testimonials; referral and partner channels; then measured acquisition. Capacity, support quality, margin, and incident rate must remain inside operating thresholds before each gate.

## Customer lifecycle

Lead → phone qualification → owner marks Closed Won → agreement → questionnaire → assets → research → strategy → architecture/design/copy/development/SEO → QA → owner approval → customer review → revision → final deployment → delivery → maintenance → renewal or controlled offboarding.

Support is human-accountable, severity-based, and recorded. Maintenance covers explicitly contracted updates, health checks, backups, security maintenance, and incident response. New scope requires a change request.

## Technology strategy

Websites use React, Next.js, TypeScript and Firebase/GCP. The control plane maintains tenant boundaries, audit history, approvals, rollbacks, backups, monitoring, and portable artifacts. Customer infrastructure may migrate to a dedicated project when risk, spend, regulation, or ownership requires it.

AI is provider-independent. Launch mode prepares tasks and prompts for manual execution; generated results are validated and re-enter the same workflow. Future API inference must be explicitly enabled, budgeted, auditable, and interchangeable without changing business stages.

## Pricing and risk philosophy

Prices cover direct labor, infrastructure, support reserve, tax/compliance overhead, risk, and target margin. Discounts require an explicit reason, limit, and owner approval; they never hide unprofitable delivery. See the financial operating system for formulas.

Risks are assigned an owner, likelihood, impact, controls, indicators, and review date. Disaster recovery targets a 24-hour RPO and four-hour RTO; restore drills use isolated infrastructure. No backup is considered valid until restoration is tested.

## Five-year vision

PageLoom becomes a profitable, operationally calm website company serving hundreds or thousands of customers through standardized delivery cells, observable infrastructure, portable customer assets, and small expert teams supervising automation. Scale is accepted only when customer outcomes and margins remain healthy.

## Decision principles and standards

1. Protect customer trust and legal obligations first.
2. Prefer a reversible, measured decision when evidence is incomplete.
3. Keep one authoritative record for customer, work, approval, finance, and incident data.
4. Never report success without evidence.
5. Automate repeated, stable work; retain owner gates for external, financial, destructive, and production actions.
6. Default customer communication to clear Hebrew; keep engineering internals in English.
7. Require typed contracts, tenant isolation, least privilege, auditability, tests, accessibility, rollback, and documentation for production changes.
