# PageLoom financial operating system

Owner: CEO · Reviewer: accountant · Review: monthly · Currency: ILS unless explicitly stated

This framework governs planning and management reporting. Tax invoices, statutory books, tax treatment, and revenue recognition require an approved Israeli accountant and compliant provider.

## Pricing and recurring revenue

Every price starts with expected delivery labor, direct infrastructure, support reserve, acquisition cost, risk contingency, and target gross margin. Setup and recurring service are priced separately. Hosting and maintenance promises must match the contracted service level. Discounts are zero-authority by default: every discount requires owner approval and a recorded commercial reason.

MRR includes active recurring commitments normalized monthly. ARR is MRR × 12. One-time setup fees are not recurring revenue. Pipeline, unsigned proposals, VAT collected, and customer deposits not yet earned are not operating revenue.

## Unit economics

- Direct customer cost = delivery labor + allocated hosting/storage/network + AI/API + payment fees + customer-specific support.
- Contribution profit = customer revenue − direct customer cost.
- Gross margin % = contribution profit ÷ customer revenue × 100.
- Fully loaded profit = contribution profit − allocated company overhead.
- Customer lifetime value = average monthly contribution profit × expected retained months.
- Break-even customers = monthly fixed costs ÷ average monthly contribution profit per active customer, rounded up.

The launch target gross margin is 60%. A quote below target is not automatically rejected, but requires owner review with an explicit strategic reason and a recovery plan.

## Forecasting and cashflow

Maintain base, downside, and upside monthly forecasts for 13 weeks and 12 months. Each separates opening cash, cash receipts, VAT/tax reserves, payroll/contractors, infrastructure, software, marketing, refunds, capital expenditure, and closing cash. Annual planning rolls the monthly base case forward and records assumption changes.

Forecast revenue uses probability-weighted signed/likely work but remains separate from actual ledgers. Cashflow uses expected payment dates, not invoice dates. Review overdue receivables weekly and never spend tax reserves as operating cash.

## Infrastructure and customer profitability

Tag every infrastructure, AI, storage, domain, and support cost to a customer where possible; otherwise allocate it using a documented driver such as active customers, traffic, storage, or delivery hours. Review customers with negative fully loaded margin monthly. Correct scope, price, automation, or service level before renewal—never silently reduce contracted service.

## Hiring assumptions

Add capacity only when the base forecast sustains the fully loaded role for six months while maintaining the cash reserve and service targets. Model salary/contract cost, employer costs, equipment, software, onboarding time, management load, and reduced initial productivity. Hiring does not substitute for fixing a broken process.

## Monthly close

1. Reconcile bank, invoices, receipts, refunds, tax reserves, and payment processors.
2. Validate revenue and expense categorization and customer/project allocation.
3. Review MRR movement, margin, cash runway, receivables, forecast variance, and infrastructure anomalies.
4. Lock the reporting period, record corrections with audit history, and publish the approved monthly report.
